# AAS Q4 Final Answer v4 - Three Practical Return Tasks

First, in year one, I will build IndoGuard, an Indonesian AI Safety Evaluation Framework for institutional AI systems. It will tackle a problem I see in practice: organisations can adopt generative AI faster than they can test whether prompts and outputs leak data, enable fraud, give harmful instructions, or mishandle slang and code-switching. The first outcome will be a safety taxonomy, small benchmark dataset, prototype checker, and risk-log template. I will measure success through tested workflows, unsafe categories detected, false-positive rate, and documented human-review decisions.

Second, in years two to three, I will turn the framework into practitioner capability. The problem is that many staff can use AI tools but do not understand prompt injection, privacy leakage, hallucination, over-blocking, or when human judgement must override AI. I will create training modules, review workshops, and a checklist for safe AI workflow design. Building on my experience mentoring more than 1,000 learners and supporting PDP awareness, I aim to train at least 500 practitioners. The outcome is not only attendance, but improved pre/post assessment scores and safer project review habits.

Third, in years three to five, I will use the scholarship network to build a wider reference for responsible AI adoption. The problem is that Indonesian institutions often work separately, so safety lessons stay inside one organisation. I will share non-sensitive findings through a benchmark report and guidance note, then invite feedback from BSSN, Komdigi, BRIN or INA Digital where appropriate, Indonesian universities, Australian academics, and AAS alumni. The target outcome is a pilot reference that SOEs and public-service teams can adapt before deployment, while strengthening Australia-Indonesia cooperation on cyber security and critical technology.

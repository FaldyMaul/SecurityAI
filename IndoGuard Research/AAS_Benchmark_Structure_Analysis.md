# AAS Benchmark Structure Analysis

Source reviewed: `Benchmark AAS Essay.pdf`

Purpose: identify the structure and strategy of the successful essay without copying its wording.

## What The Benchmark Does Well

### 1. It starts each answer with a clear problem

The benchmark does not begin with a generic love of study. It starts with a national problem, gives concrete data, then links the problem to the applicant's career.

How to adapt:

- Start from Indonesia's AI safety and cyber security problem.
- Use BSSN, OJK, IndoSafety, and AAS priority evidence.
- Connect the problem to Telkom AI Playground, National AI Sandbox, and sovereign AI infrastructure work.
- Add one human trigger from your direct work so the answer does not read like a policy memo.

### 2. It links the course to one specific mission

The benchmark does not list subjects randomly. It chooses course elements that support one clear mission.

How to adapt:

- UTS NLP sub-major: local-language safety classification.
- The Ethics of Data and AI: responsible AI deployment and public trust.
- Technology and Innovation Management: translating research responsibly into institutional practice.
- Technology Research Preparation plus Industry/Research Project: evaluation benchmark and research prototype for Indonesian AI safety.

### 3. It uses measurable targets

The benchmark includes targets such as launch timelines, number of beneficiaries, and percentage improvement.

How to adapt:

- Research prototype within 12 months after return.
- Controlled evaluation in Telkom AI Playground or National AI Sandbox.
- Train 500 practitioners within three years.
- Test 5 safety categories across formal Indonesian, informal Indonesian, Indo-English, and one regional-language feasibility sample if available.

Only use targets you can defend in an interview.

### 4. It gives a leadership example with a before-and-after result

The benchmark uses a professional project where the applicant led people, solved a real institutional problem, and delivered a measurable result.

Best examples from your CV:

- AI-based talent matching for BP BUMN: improved mapping accuracy by 35 percent.
- WAD Workload Analysis and Talent Management System: won INAICA 2025 Operational Excellence Award.
- Personal Data Protection Law Task Force: strengthened governance and compliance in enterprise digital products.
- Inalyst: scaled to more than 1,000 enterprise users and more than IDR1 billion recorded revenue.

Recommended strongest leadership example:

Use the BP BUMN or WAD example if the question asks for measurable institutional reform. Use PDP Task Force if the question asks directly about cyber security, governance, or risk.

### 5. It has a practical reintegration plan

The benchmark gives three practical return tasks, each with a pathway and partner.

How to adapt:

- Task 1: Build and evaluate an Indonesian AI safety research prototype in Telkom's AI platform environment.
- Task 2: Train AI engineers, product managers, and public-sector teams in responsible AI deployment.
- Task 3: Collaborate with BSSN, Komdigi, BRIN, INA Digital, universities, and Australian alumni to expand the safety benchmark and adoption pathway.

### 6. It anticipates constraints

The benchmark does not pretend the plan is easy. It names technical, outreach, and bureaucracy barriers, then gives mitigation.

How to adapt:

- Data limitation: start narrow, use human verification and native speakers, and avoid promising multiple regional languages too early.
- False positives: risk scoring, human review, and evaluation by language variety.
- Bureaucracy: start with Telkom pilot and use evidence before wider adoption.
- Censorship concern: rights-preserving framework, transparent taxonomy, audit trail, review/appeal, and human oversight.
- Compute cost: compact classifier, sandbox testing, high-risk category prioritisation.

## Recommended Tone

The benchmark tone is:

- Personal but not sentimental.
- Ambitious but still practical.
- Technical enough to be credible.
- Focused on public impact.
- Clear about the applicant's role, not only the organisation's role.

For your draft, avoid:

- Overly polished AI-like phrasing.
- Buzzword stacking.
- Claims that Indonesia has no AI safety work at all.
- Too many acronyms without explaining the public value.
- A plan that sounds like a national project without a first pilot.
- Sounding like a corporate product proposal.
- Sounding over-qualified or as if the master's is just a credential.
- Making Telkom's operational benefit louder than the public good.

## Best Structure To Follow

For each AAS answer:

1. Problem in Indonesia.
2. Your direct experience with the problem.
3. What you did or plan to do.
4. The academic or theoretical gap you cannot solve through work experience alone.
5. Why Australia/UTS fills that missing capability.
6. Measurable return impact.
7. Realistic constraints and mitigation, if the question asks.

"I have already worked on AI systems and AI security infrastructure inside Telkom. As Indonesia begins deploying sovereign AI platforms, I want to specialise in AI safety so these systems can be trusted in Bahasa Indonesia, informal speech, local languages, and public-sector contexts. Studying AI at UTS will help me build the technical and ethical capability to create an Indonesian AI guardrail that protects institutions and citizens from data leakage, fraud, harmful prompts, misinformation, and unsafe outputs."

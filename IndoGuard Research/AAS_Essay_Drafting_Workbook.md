# AAS Essay Drafting Workbook

This is not a final essay. Do not submit this file as an application answer. Use it to write your own answers in your own words, consistent with `AI_rules.md`.

The benchmark PDF uses a clear pattern:

- Start with a real national problem.
- Connect the problem to the applicant's career and personal motivation.
- Explain why the course is the missing bridge.
- Show measurable post-study impact.
- Use specific leadership examples with results.
- Give practical return plans and constraints.

- Add one brief human trigger from your real experience before the technical explanation.

## Question 1: Why did you choose your proposed course and institution?

Your answer should do four things:

- Name UTS Master of Artificial Intelligence as first preference.
- Start from the Indonesia problem: safe generative AI adoption, not AI hype.
- Explain why UTS is technically relevant: NLP, Ethics of Data and AI, Technology and Innovation Management, research/industry project.
- Show that this is linked to your current Telkom work and future Indonesian impact.

Evidence you can use:

- AAS priority fields include Strong Institutions, Cyber security, Misinformation and disinformation, and Research and innovation.
- Llama Guard 3-1B supports safety classification for inputs and outputs but does not list Indonesian as a supported language.
- IndoSafety shows Indonesian-centric LLMs can generate unsafe outputs, especially in colloquial and local-language settings.
- UTS offers an NLP sub-major and ethics/project subjects.
- UNESCO, OECD, and NIST provide ethical and risk-management anchors for human oversight, transparency, accountability, and risk measurement.

Write in your own words from these prompts:

- What is one specific moment in your AI Playground, AI Sandbox, PDP, or LLM work that made you feel the risk was no longer theoretical?
- Did you see a model mishandle personal data, follow an unsafe prompt, fail on informal Indonesian, or produce a confident answer that could mislead a non-technical user?
- How did that moment make you feel responsible rather than merely interested?
- What did you personally see at Telkom that made AI safety feel urgent?
- When leading AI Playground, AI Sandbox, or National AI Inference Platform work, what risk worried you most?
- Why is "guardrail for Indonesian AI" more precise than simply building another AI platform?
- Which UTS subjects are directly useful, and what will you build or research with them?
- Why is Australia the right place, given Australia-Indonesia cyber and critical technology cooperation?
- What is the academic wall you have hit: NLP safety evaluation, dataset annotation, adversarial code-switching, calibration, or rights-preserving governance?

Human trigger worksheet:

- Where were you when it happened?
- What system or workflow were you testing, without revealing confidential details?
- What did the model do wrong?
- Who could be harmed if the same failure happened in a public-service setting?
- What did that moment reveal about the limits of your current training?

Use only one sentence or one short paragraph. Do not dramatise it beyond what actually happened.

Possible paragraph structure:

1. Personal trigger from a real professional moment.
2. Indonesia problem and public-risk evidence.
3. Technical gap: Indonesian-language AI safety evaluation.
3. Why UTS first preference.
4. Why the study is necessary despite your current senior role.
5. How the study serves Indonesian public institutions and citizens.

Do not copy this sentence directly, but this is the idea:

Indonesia is entering a phase where generative AI will be used in public services and strategic industries, but Indonesia still lacks a rigorous way to evaluate safety failures in formal Bahasa Indonesia, informal language, and code-switching before these systems reach citizens.

- Result: won INAICA 2025 Operational Excellence Award.
- AAI link: institutional effectiveness, digital transformation, research and innovation.

Option C: Personal Data Protection Law Task Force

- Challenge: new national legal and ethical data obligations.
- Action: helped ensure enterprise digital products met PDP standards.
- Result: stronger data governance in Telkom digital products.
- AAI link: cyber security, governance, strong institutions.

Option D: Inalyst

- Challenge: limited practical data analytics adoption.
- Action: built startup inside Telkom ecosystem, served B2B clients, created community.
- Result: more than 1,000 active enterprise users and more than IDR1 billion recorded revenue.

Recommended structure:

- Situation: What was broken or risky?
- Task: What responsibility did you take?
- Action: What did you personally do, especially leadership and influencing?
- Collaboration: Who did you work with?
- Result: What changed, with numbers?
- Reflection: What did it teach you that connects to AI safety?

Reviewer note:

- Downplay the "corporate success" tone.
- Emphasise what the change meant for fairness, accountability, transparency, public trust, or institutional quality.
- If using the BP BUMN example, focus on reducing subjectivity in leadership mapping, not just technical accuracy.
- If using the PDP Task Force example, focus on citizen privacy and institutional responsibility, not compliance box-ticking.

Corporate-to-public-good translation:

| Improved accuracy | Reduced subjectivity and supported fairer decision-making |
| Generated revenue | Proved ability to move ideas into adoption; keep revenue secondary |

## Question 5: Constraints that may prevent achievement and mitigation

Write this honestly. Strong answers show you understand implementation risk.

Constraint 1: lack of high-quality local-language safety data.

Mitigation:

- Start with formal and informal Indonesian.
- Add only one regional-language proof of concept through native-speaker review.
- Use human verification for sensitive examples.

Constraint 2: false positives and false negatives.

Mitigation:

- Use risk scoring, not automatic blocking, for ambiguous cases.
- Create a human review workflow.
- Evaluate by language variety and harm category.

Constraint 3: adoption barriers in government or SOEs.

Mitigation:

- Start with Telkom internal platforms.
- Show measurable pilot outcomes.
- Engage compliance, security, legal, and product teams early.

Constraint 4: concern that guardrails become censorship.

Mitigation:

- Anchor the system in UNESCO, OECD, and NIST principles: human oversight, transparency, accountability, safety, privacy, and risk management.
- Treat ambiguous claims as "review required", not "false" or "blocked".
- Keep transparent taxonomy, audit logs, and human oversight.
- Separate public-interest risk scoring from political opinion filtering.
- Measure false positives and over-blocking as a core research outcome.

Constraint 5: compute and operational cost.

Mitigation:

- Begin with compact classifiers or fine-tuned open models.
- Use the AI Sandbox for controlled testing.
- Prioritise high-risk categories first.

# AAS Q5 Final Answer v4 - Constraints

The first constraint is access to safe and representative data. Real prompts or business cases may contain personal or confidential information. I will reduce this risk by using anonymised or synthetic examples, data-governance approval, and clear separation between sensitive operational data and publishable benchmark findings.

The second constraint is people and annotation quality. Indonesian safety labels need reviewers who understand formal language, slang, code-switching, social context, and privacy risk. I will start with a small trained reviewer group, written annotation guidance, and inter-reviewer checks before expanding. I will also use Australian academic networks, Indonesian universities, and AAS alumni to find reviewers and advisers with NLP, ethics, and cyber security expertise.

The third constraint is resources. A full guardrail model may need GPU capacity, data engineering, and sustained technical maintenance. I will begin with a realistic evaluation framework, benchmark dataset, risk taxonomy, and lightweight prototype before any large fine-tuning. If the evidence is strong, I will seek institutional support, research collaboration, and shared compute resources.

The fourth constraint is over-blocking and censorship risk. A safety checker can wrongly flag lawful discussion or sensitive civic debate. I will design IndoGuard as risk triage, not automatic censorship, with transparent categories, human review, false-positive measurement, and guidance from UNESCO and OECD principles.

The fifth constraint is partnership and adoption. Institutions may hesitate to share failures or change existing workflows. I will start with non-sensitive findings, pilot champions, practical guidance notes, and regular review workshops. The aim is to show that AI safety protects citizens, staff, and institutional trust, not just compliance.

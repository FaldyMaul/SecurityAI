# AAS Strict Reviewer Refinement

Purpose: convert the feedback from the "cruel reviewer" into concrete changes for the AAS strategy.

This file is not application content. It is a review-risk checklist.

## Main Strategic Shift

Old framing:

- Build an Indonesian AI Guardrail MVP for sovereign AI infrastructure.

Refined framing:

- Research and validate an Indonesian AI safety evaluation framework for sovereign AI systems.

Why this matters:

- The old version sounded like corporate product development.
- The refined version sounds like a master's-level academic problem with practical development impact.
- It makes UTS/RMIT study necessary because the missing capability is not basic engineering; it is rigorous NLP evaluation, annotation methodology, model auditing, and responsible AI governance.

## Revised Core Idea

The proposed study should investigate how AI safety guardrails perform on Indonesian-language generative AI interactions, especially:

- Formal Bahasa Indonesia.
- Informal Bahasa Indonesia.
- Slang and online expression.
- Indo-English code-switching.
- Prompt injection and jailbreak attempts.
- Personal data leakage.
- Fraud and scam enablement.
- Misinformation risk requiring human verification.

The master's project should aim for:

- A localized safety taxonomy.
- A human-verified evaluation set.
- A benchmark comparing existing guardrails.
- A research prototype or adapted classifier.
- An ethical governance model for human review and auditability.

It should not promise a full national deployment during the master's.

## Academic Novelty Angle

The proposal becomes more academically credible if it asks:

- How do existing global and regional guardrails fail in Indonesian contexts?
- Which errors are caused by register, slang, cultural ambiguity, or code-switching?
- How can a safety taxonomy avoid conflating public-interest speech with harmful content?
- How can over-blocking be measured alongside unsafe-content detection?
- What minimum governance conditions are needed before a guardrail is used in public-sector AI systems?

This is stronger than:

- "I will fine-tune Qwen-SEA-Guard."
- "I will build an audit dashboard."
- "I will make a national AI guardrail."

## Censorship Dilemma Response

The essay must explicitly answer: who decides what is unsafe?

Use this logic:

- The model should not decide political truth.
- The model should classify operational safety risk.
- Ambiguous categories should be routed to human review.
- Misinformation should be treated as "verification required", not automatic suppression.
- The taxonomy should be transparent and contestable.
- Public-sector deployment must include audit logs and accountability.

Ethical anchors:

- UNESCO: human rights, human dignity, transparency, fairness, human oversight.
- OECD: human-centric AI, transparency, robustness, safety, accountability.
- NIST AI RMF: govern, map, measure, manage risk.

## Over-Qualification Response

Risk:

- The panel may think the applicant is already too advanced for a coursework master's.

Answer:

- Current experience proves readiness, not completion.
- The applicant can build AI systems, but still lacks deep academic training in multilingual NLP safety evaluation and algorithmic auditing.
- National-scale AI deployment raises the cost of blind spots.
- The master's is necessary because the applicant is close enough to deployment to know that self-learning is no longer enough.

Use in drafting:

- Be humble.
- Admit limits clearly.
- Do not sound like "I already know this and just need a degree."
- Sound like "I have enough responsibility to know what I do not yet know."

## Public Good Response

Risk:

- The application may sound like Telkom corporate R&D.

Fix:

- Telkom is the platform and deployment environment.
- The beneficiary is the Indonesian public.
- The development outcome is safer digital public services, stronger institutional trust, better AI governance, reduced fraud enablement, and reduced privacy leakage.
- Avoid making revenue, enterprise efficiency, or corporate competitiveness the main impact.

Use Telkom examples only to prove:

- Access to implementation environment.
- Ability to lead teams.
- Ability to move from prototype to adoption.
- Realistic return-to-Indonesia plan.

## Local-Language Scope Correction

Old risk:

- Promising Javanese, Sundanese, and Minangkabau too quickly sounds naive.

Refined scope:

- Master's focus: formal Indonesian, informal Indonesian, and Indo-English code-switching.
- Optional proof of concept: one regional language, only if native-speaker annotation is feasible.
- Long-term ambition: broader regional-language coverage through partnerships.

## Human Voice Requirement

The final essay should include one brief, real moment from the applicant's experience.

Good trigger types:

- A model mishandled a prompt in informal Indonesian.
- A sandbox test exposed a prompt-injection or data-leakage risk.
- A public-sector or SOE use case showed that unsafe AI output could affect real people.
- PDP work made privacy risk feel personal rather than abstract.
- A non-technical stakeholder trusted an AI output too easily.

Rules:

- Keep the anecdote short.
- Make it specific.
- Do not disclose confidential details.
- Use it to explain motivation, then move into evidence and course fit.

## Red Lines

Avoid saying:

- "I will build the national guardrail."
- "This will solve misinformation."
- "The model will decide whether content is true."
- "I will cover Javanese, Sundanese, and Minangkabau in the first year."
- "Telkom will benefit from my new AI product."
- "There is no Indonesian AI safety work yet."

Safer alternatives:

- "I will develop and evaluate a prototype."
- "The model will support risk triage and human review."
- "The research will measure both unsafe outputs and over-blocking."
- "I will begin with formal/informal Indonesian and code-switching."
- "Telkom provides a realistic test environment, while the intended benefit is safer AI for Indonesian institutions and citizens."
- "Existing work has begun, but Indonesia still needs deeper national adaptation and institutional evaluation."

UTS is also strong beyond the course structure. Its Australian Artificial Intelligence Institute works across explainable AI, text mining, and AI privacy and security, while the Human Technology Institute and Data Science Institute provide strong references for responsible AI governance and ethical AI.

To choose this option, I compared the UTS and RMIT course structures, reviewed UTS Handbook subjects, mapped them against my proposed AI safety project, and studied Llama Guard 4, IndoSafety, SEA-Guard, Australia's AI Safety Standard, and the Australia-Indonesia cyber cooperation agenda. ## Question 5

**List any possible constraints you think may prevent you from achieving the above tasks.**

The first constraint is high-quality Indonesian safety data. Harmful prompts may appear in formal Indonesian, slang, code-switching, or regional linguistic context. Real examples may also be sensitive. I will start with synthetic and anonymised test cases, adapt public research such as IndoSafety only where appropriate, and use human-in-the-loop verification before expanding to regional-language coverage.

The second constraint is annotation quality. A localized guardrail depends on consistent labels, but Indonesian safety issues can involve culture, religion, politics, privacy, and public-service context. I will develop clear annotation guidelines, involve native speakers or domain reviewers where feasible, and measure false positives as seriously as unsafe outputs so the tool does not become a blunt censorship layer.

The third constraint is infrastructure and research boundary. A Llama Guard 4-style model may require GPU resources, secure storage, and careful separation between academic research data and Telkom operational data. I will begin with evaluation and benchmarking rather than full model training, use the controlled sandbox for limited pilots, and only move to fine-tuning if data governance, compute access, and ethical review are sufficient.

The fourth constraint is institutional adoption. SOEs and government agencies may resist AI safety protocols if they seem complex, costly, or unproven. I will reduce this risk by producing practical evidence first: a pilot benchmark, prototype results, training modules, and a short guidance note. I will frame the work as shared public value and cyber resilience, not only as Telkom's internal product.

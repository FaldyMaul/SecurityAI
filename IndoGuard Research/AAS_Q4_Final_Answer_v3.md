# AAS Q4 Final Answer v3 - Three Practical Return Tasks

First, in year one, I will develop an Indonesian-language AI safety checker inspired by Llama Guard-style systems and integrate it as a review layer in AgentLab and ModelHub. The tool will check user prompts and AI outputs for privacy leakage, harmful instructions, fraud enablement, misinformation risk, and unsafe local-language content. The outputs will be a safety taxonomy, benchmark dataset, prototype, and risk logs that AI product teams can review before internal release.

Second, in years two to three, I will create safe-AI workflow training for employees and practitioners. AgentLab makes AI development faster, so users must also understand data leakage, unsafe outputs, over-blocking, and human review. I will develop training modules, review workshops, and a practical checklist for teams building with AgentLab and ModelHub. Building on my experience mentoring more than 1,000 learners, I aim to train at least 500 practitioners through Telkom learning channels, online sessions, and AAS or academic networks.

Third, in years three to five, I will share non-sensitive findings with broader institutions. I will prepare a benchmark report and guidance note, then invite feedback from BSSN, Komdigi, BRIN or INA Digital where appropriate, Indonesian universities, Australian academics, and AAS alumni. This will begin as a pilot reference, not a national standard. The aim is to help SOEs and public-service teams understand how to evaluate AI workflows before deployment, while supporting Australia-Indonesia cooperation on cyber security and critical technology.
